01 Introduction

Rucaa is a multi-vendor marketplace for fashion and apparel, open only to authentic, homegrown Indian brands.

Its purpose is to give India's craftsmanship a wider stage: a single destination where independent labels can reach customers far beyond their own city, and where buyers can trust that what they find is genuinely made here.

The audience values the story behind a piece as much as the piece itself. They look for quality, originality and cultural identity, and they choose brands that reflect their own values.

The name joins two Sanskrit-derived words into one idea: the beauty and light that come from within. It shifts the brand's attention from surface to source.

For a marketplace of Indian makers, that idea works twice. It speaks to the wearer, whose style is an expression of who they are, and to the brands, whose value lies in the craft and intention behind what they make.

03 The creative challenge

Represent Indian fashion without reaching for its clichés.

Paisleys, temple borders and ethnic ornament would have placed Rucaa instantly, and made it interchangeable. Conventional fashion branding would have made it generic. The task was to turn an abstract phrase, Beauty Within, into a mark that is recognisable, considered and genuinely its own.

04 Inspiration

A natural form for an inner change.

Rather than start from fashion symbols, the search began with a question: what in nature shows beauty emerging from within? The butterfly answers it more directly than anything else. Its beauty is not applied from outside. It is the result of transformation.

05 Research and exploration

Studying a butterfly's structure, not its picture.

The Queen Alexandra's birdwing became the specific reference. Its long forewings, clear symmetry and deliberate proportions read almost like drawn lines, which made it a strong starting point for typography.

Looking at a natural form for its structure, rather than reproducing its image, is what keeps a mark original. The goal was to understand the contour of one wing well enough to translate it into a controlled, typographic stroke.

06 · The design journey

Two wings, one body.

Mirrored about a single vertical stroke, the wing becomes a butterfly. This is the form the letter grew from, and the one it returns to across the identity.

Folding the form

Keep one wing. Keep the stem.

Take away the mirrored half and what remains is a single wing joined to a vertical: the anatomy of an R.

Organic geometry, typographic expression

The wing becomes the letter.

The forewing's contour draws the R's upper stroke. The hindwing sweeps down into its leg. The butterfly is not placed beside the name. It is built into the first letter of it.

The wordmark

A signature initial, set with restraint.

The custom R leads; the remaining letters are set in Lavish by Epdesigns, whose fine contrast carries the R's calligraphic energy without competing with it.
